Where the work sits, domain by domain

A rough placement of the 2026 monograph against nine neighbouring fields: how close it comes to each field's current state, and the level of expertise the writing and the citations suggest. The profile is T-shaped: expert in uncertainty, reliability and decision, broadly read across philosophy, computing and ML, and least well positioned in the two fields nearest the 2026 thesis.

"Forefront" for uncertainty quantification applies to one point: ontological uncertainty and the frame as a common cause in quantitative practice. "Forefront" for the research method refers to practice, since the build is not yet written up. The outlined cell is your home field.

Your strongest card, and the risk around it. Expertise in uncertainty and reliability is what lets the book carry reliability logic over to checks on reasoning. Two neighbouring fields have studied "independent checks that can fail differently" for years under other names: safety science (defences in depth, control structures) and AI oversight (scalable oversight, AI control, monoculture). A reviewer from either will ask first how the book differs from them.

The answer exists and is yours: the frame acts as a common cause of every internal check, and ontological uncertainty stays out of sight by construction. Neither field states this as sharply. The book does not yet make the comparison.

Domain by domain

Uncertainty quantification & reliability

Forefront, one pointExpert · home field

CoversDer Kiureghian & Ditlevsen and Oberkampf & Roy on kinds of uncertainty, Knight 1921, Walker 2003, Lempert 2003, value of information. The ontological-uncertainty argument goes further than most UQ papers.

GapThe design-diversity models of software reliability (Eckhardt–Lee 1985, Littlewood–Miller 1989), which are the formal theory of "failing differently". Common-cause failure models from probabilistic safety assessment, such as the beta factor; M1's shared-cause parameter is close to them.

Forefront, as practiceAdvanced practitioner

Bayesian decision theory

Mainstream, solidAdvanced to expert

Philosophy of science

State of the art, in pocketsWell-read practitioner

CoversMcCarthy & Hayes, Dennett and Shanahan on the frame problem; Hume, Goodman; Mayo 1996 and 2018 on severity; robustness analysis (Levins, Wimsatt, Orzack); Wynne 1992 on ignorance. Carrying the frame problem into quantitative practice is a relatively original niche.

GapSocial epistemology (testimony, epistemic dependence), which the chapter on collective blind spots touches.

Safety science & human factors

Lagging in positioningInformed practitioner

CoversBainbridge 1983, Tufte and the Rogers commission on Challenger, Knight Capital.

GapReason's defences in depth, Perrow's normal accidents, Leveson's control-structure view, Parasuraman on complacency, Mosier on automation bias, Lee & See on trust in automation. The five conditions and the placement of checks overlap strongly with this literature.

AI safety & oversight

Lagging, where the thesis lands hardestInformed generalist

CoversChristiano 2017 and Ouyang 2022 only.

GapScalable oversight, debate, AI control, weak-to-strong supervision, and the self-preference of models judging their own output. "A checker that fails differently from the producer" is this field's central question, so without explicit positioning the book risks being read as rediscovering it.
