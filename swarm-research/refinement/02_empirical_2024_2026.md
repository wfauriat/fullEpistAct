## 2. Cluster 1 Deep Dive: Skill Erosion & Cognitive Debt

### Verified Primary Findings & Caveats
* **Budzyń et al. (2025)** (*The Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology*, Oct 2025; online Aug 2025) provides the strongest clinical field signal of post-AI vigilance decay. Across four Polish endoscopy centres ($N = 1,443$ standard non-AI colonoscopies: $795$ pre-AI, $648$ post-AI), experienced clinicians' unaided adenoma detection rate (ADR) dropped from **28.4%** ($226/795$) to **22.4%** ($145/648$)—an absolute difference of **$-6.0\%$** (95% CI $-10.5$ to $-1.6$, $p = 0.0089$; multivariable $\text{OR} = 0.69$, 95% CI $0.53$–$0.89$). Crucial caveats: non-randomized observational design (vulnerable to temporal or patient-mix shifts), limited per-centre statistical power for operator subgroups, restriction to experienced endoscopists, and short post-exposure blocks that cannot separate lasting skill atrophy from transient attentional adaptation.
* **Bastani, Bastani, Sungu et al.** (*PNAS*, `[UNVERIFIED exact publication year: 2024/2025]`) isolates causal skill impairment in a three-arm randomized field trial of **nearly 1,000 high-school math students** (`[UNVERIFIED exact integer N]`). Unguarded **GPT Base** boosted practice scores **$+48\%$** above control, yet degraded subsequent unassisted exam scores by **$-17\%$**. Conversely, a Socratic **GPT Tutor** delivering teacher-designed hints boosted practice scores **$+127\%$** while preserving unassisted exam parity with controls. Caveats: single-domain high-school mathematics, immediate post-removal assessment (no multi-month retention), and sensitivity to students bypassing constrained interfaces when unguarded AI is accessible.
* **Lee, Sarkar, Tankelevitch, Drosos, Rintel, Banks & Wilson (2025)** (*ACM CHI 2025*) surveyed **$N = 319$ knowledge workers** on Prolific across **$936$ real-world GenAI tasks**. Higher confidence in GenAI predicted **lower** self-reported critical thinking effort, whereas higher task-specific self-confidence predicted **greater** critical thinking. Workers reported shifting effort from initial drafting/structuring toward verification, response integration, and task stewardship. Caveats: retrospective self-reports (not objective cognitive telemetry), cross-sectional design (cannot prove skill atrophy), and Prolific tech-literacy bias.

### Discrepancies & Over-Extrapolations Found
1. **Causal overstatement on Budzyń et al.:** `synthesis.md` (line 78) asserts routine AI reliance *"measurably erodes unaided vigilance (Budzyń et al. 2025)"*. While `research/09_human_factors_oversight.md` (line 20) rightly flags non-randomization, `synthesis.md` flattens an observational association into causal erosion.
2. **Omitted `+127%` Tutor effect:** `cross_review_monograph.md` and `research/09` report Bastani's $+48\%$ and $-17\%$ figures accurately, but omit the **$+127\%$** practice gain under GPT Tutor (and leave exact $N$/year unverified).
3. **Blind spot in `livre.html`:** Ch. 15.4 invokes Bainbridge (1983) conceptually without citing Budzyń, Bastani, or Lee et al.—missing both the empirical validation of cognitive debt and Bastani's demonstration that *pedagogical scaffolding* prevents exam decay.

## 3. Cluster 2 Deep Dive: Human–AI Synergy, Calibration & Perception

### Verified Primary Findings & Caveats
* **Vaccaro, Almaatouq & Malone (2024)** (*Nature Human Behaviour*) pooled **$106$ experimental studies** ($370$ effect sizes) and found human–AI teams performed significantly **worse** than the best solo agent (**Hedges’ $g = -0.23$**, 95% CI $[-0.39, -0.07]$). Crucially, performance diverged by task and baseline asymmetry: **decision-making** tasks and cases where **AI outperformed humans alone** produced losses, whereas **content creation** and cases where **humans outperformed AI alone** yielded genuine synergy. Limitations include possible publication bias, wide cross-disciplinary heterogeneity in models/interfaces, and predominantly short-term lab settings.
* **Steyvers, Smyth et al. (2025)** (*Nature Machine Intelligence*, *"What large language models know and what people think they know"*) tested GPT-3.5, PaLM 2, and GPT-4o across multiple-choice and short-answer QA with **$N = 301$ US native English participants** ($284$ complete demographics). Users systematically overestimated default LLM accuracy, worsened by a **length bias** in which longer, fluent explanations raised perceived confidence without improving correctness. Aligning explanation wording to internal model confidence narrowed both the calibration gap and discrimination gap. Caveats: modest US crowd sample, structured QA rather than open coding/synthesis, single-turn judgments, and prompt/RLHF sensitivity.
* **Kim et al. (2024)** (*ACM FAccT 2024*, *"I’m Not Sure, But..."*) ran a pre-registered experiment (**$N = 404$**) on medical search decisions, showing that first-person uncertainty hedges lowered blind trust and agreement while **improving overall user accuracy** by curbing overreliance—though overreliance was reduced rather than eliminated, and longitudinal habituation remains untested.
* **Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos (2021)** (*Proc. ACM HCI / CSCW*, $N = 199$) demonstrated that three Cognitive Forcing Functions cut overreliance on wrong AI suggestions compared with passive XAI rationales, **at the cost of the lowest subjective user satisfaction** and uneven gains favoring participants higher in *Need for Cognition*.
* **Becker, Rush, Barnes & Rein (2025)** (`arXiv:2507.09089`, METR), verified in `livre.html` and `research/09`, tracked **$N = 16$ experienced OSS developers** across **$246$ real tasks** on mature repositories ($\sim 5$ years familiarity) using Cursor Pro (Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet). Objective completion time **increased by $+19\%$** (95% CI $+2\%$ to $+39\%$), inverting pre-trial forecasts ($24\%$ speedup; experts $38$–$39\%$) and post-trial beliefs ($20\%$ speedup).

### Discrepancies & Over-Extrapolations Found
1. **Venue misattribution for Kim et al. (2024):** Both `cross_review_monograph.md` (line 52) and `research/09` (line 137) misattribute Kim et al. (2024) to ***ACM CHI***. Primary-source records confirm it was published at ***ACM FAccT 2024***.
2. **Preprint vs. published title for Steyvers et al. (2025):** `research/09` (line 150) cites the arXiv preprint title (*"The calibration gap between model and human confidence in large language models"*); the *Nature Machine Intelligence* title is ***"What large language models know and what people think they know"***.
3. **External-validity bounds on Becker et al. (2025):** `livre.html` (Ch. 15, lines 10998–11002) appropriately restricts the $+19\%$ magnitude to early-2025 tooling and protocol. Given $N = 16$ developers on deeply familiar codebases, extrapolating that exact slowdown to unfamiliar codebases or junior engineers would overreach, though the *sign inversion* between subjective belief and measured latency directly corroborates Ch. 15's argument.

## 4. Cluster 3 Deep Dive: Homogenization, Monoculture & Institutional Bottlenecks

### Verified Primary Findings & Caveats
* **Doshi & Hauser (2024)** (*Science Advances*) randomized **$n = 300$ non-professional creators** to write 8-sentence micro-stories rated by **$n = 600$ human evaluators**. GenAI ideas raised individual novelty, usefulness, and enjoyment (especially for less creative writers) while **sharply reducing collective diversity** across the output pool (`[UNVERIFIED exact effect deltas]`). Caveats: brief micro-story task, crowd writers, and single-shot ideation.
* **Liang et al. (2023 & 2024) — Two Distinct Papers:**
  * **Liang et al. (2023, *Patterns*):** Audited seven GPT detectors on essays including **$91$ TOEFL non-native essays** vs. US 8th-grade native essays, documenting a **61.22%** overall false-positive rate on TOEFL writing (up to **97%+** on individual detectors) vs. near-zero false positives on native 8th-grade essays.
  * **Liang et al. (2024):** Applied maximum-likelihood lexical estimation to AI conference peer reviews across **ICLR 2024, NeurIPS 2023, CoRL 2023, and EMNLP 2023**, estimating **6.5%–16.9%** of review text was substantially LLM-modified (`[UNVERIFIED exact review N and publication venue ICLR vs. ICML]`).
* **Wataoka et al. (2024/2025)** (`arXiv`, *"Self-Preference Bias in LLM-as-a-Judge"*) established that LLM judges systematically favor **low-perplexity** outputs matching their own probability distributions regardless of true authorship (`[UNVERIFIED exact N and correlation coefficients]`).
* **Shumailov et al. (2024) vs. Gerstgrasser et al. (2024):** While Shumailov et al. (2024, *Nature* 631: 755–759) proved Early and Late Model Collapse under recursive **replacement**, **Gerstgrasser, Schaeffer, Koyejo et al. (2024)** (*"Is Model Collapse Inevitable?"*) proved across transformers, VAEs, and diffusion models that **accumulating** synthetic data alongside a retained real-data anchor keeps test error upper-bounded and prevents catastrophic collapse.
* **Live RAG Contamination & OSS AI-Slop Triage (2025–2026):** Targeted search confirms emerging empirical documentation of **"Retrieval Collapse"**—where synthetic SEO/AI text flooding the web narrows retrieved source diversity in RAG pipelines and traps models in self-referential loops—and widespread OSS maintainer backlashes against automated PRs and hallucinated bug reports (*curl* ending its bug-bounty pipeline, *Ghostty* contribution gating, Python libraries, and *tldraw* cited in `research/08`). However, **primary-source author lists, sample sizes, and exact quantitative merge-rate or retrieval-collapse metrics remain `[UNVERIFIED]`** in retrieved summaries.

### Discrepancies & Over-Extrapolations Found
1. **Unconditional collapse in `livre.html` (Ch. 16.3, lines 11884–11892):** `livre.html` presents Shumailov et al. (2024) without Gerstgrasser et al.’s (2024) *replacement vs. accumulation* qualification, overstating unconditional pretraining doom while missing the sharper operational threat: **live RAG/web contamination** on post-2023 facts lacking provenance.
2. **Conflation & venue errors on Liang et al.:** `cross_review_monograph.md` (lines 53, 60) and `research/08` (line 20) cite *"Liang et al. (2024)"* for non-native detector bias (actually **2023, *Patterns***). Meanwhile, `research/08` (line 67) states the peer-review corpus covered *"ICLR, ICML"*, whereas verified primary summaries list **ICLR 2024, NeurIPS 2023, CoRL 2023, and EMNLP 2023** (`ICML` inclusion `[UNVERIFIED]`).
3. **Unverified "ownership penalty" attribution:** `research/09` (line 87) and `cross_review_monograph.md` (line 54) attribute an *"ownership penalty"* against disclosed AI assistance to Doshi & Hauser (2024); that result was **`[UNVERIFIED]`** in retrieved Doshi & Hauser summaries and likely stems from adjacent disclosure literature.
4. **Unverified quantitative claims in `research/08`:** References to the EMNLP 2025 **DBG score** (lines 49, 109) and *"empirical analyses of millions of GitHub PRs"* (line 66) lack verified primary author/sample metadata in this audit and should be tagged `[UNVERIFIED]` pending direct primary-paper inspection.
